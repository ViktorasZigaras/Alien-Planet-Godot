# Alien Planet — four stages of play

Design synthesis • direction round completed 2 November 2026

## What this document concludes

The game taking shape is an opportunity-driven planetary civilization game. Geography offers possibilities; persistent people supply the labour and military force; infrastructure creates capacity; research changes what is feasible; the leader chooses which opportunities and problems deserve resources. The desired AI should carry that chain through deterministically, in whatever supported situation it encounters.

Your answers suggest a coherent progression: **establish a viable foothold → grow a protected network → specialize and change how the civilization operates → pursue chosen ideological or planetary ambitions.** Security and development continue throughout. These are overlapping emphases, not four compulsory scripts, turn ranges or mutually exclusive strategies.

This document completes the first interview's synthesis request, groups the ideas into four actionable bins and adds a filtered set of possibilities from the collected SMAC/SMAX, Beyond Earth/Rising Tide and Alien Planet AI material. It is a design proposal for review. Stage placement, usefulness judgments and new combinations below are assistant judgments; an earlier user decision retains its original firmness or tentativeness.

The agreed order is: **this synthesis → another round of direction questions → comparison with the existing build → an agreed repair/addition plan.** No game code, simulations, new scenarios, log deletions or implementation batch are authorized by this document. Test scenarios require your suggestion or approval. The later comparison must use the actual available source and distinguish it from historical reports.

## Reading the bins

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Essential | Carries the central loop or prevents that loop becoming incoherent. High design relevance; not automatically the next coding task. |
| High | Adds a strong recurring choice or makes existing choices more meaningful. |
| Conditional | Useful if it solves a demonstrated problem; keep until the need is clear. |
| Exploratory | Interesting future direction; prerequisites, payoff or scope are still uncertain. |
| Decision | Clear user direction, with unspecified details still open. |
| Provisional | Accepted as a starting assumption or test value, not settled balance. |
| Possibility | Tentative, conditional or deferred user idea. |
| Recommendation | Assistant-selected addition or interpretation from the collected sources; awaits discussion. |

Each row gives an action the design or a later test can address, its usefulness and its source. **An actionable idea can be a question, a rule to specify or a capability to demonstrate; it does not have to be a new building.** IDs such as GS04 and M08 let subsequent discussion refer to an exact item. Source labels S1–S10 link to the source register at the end; Q IDs refer to the original decision record.

Stage readiness is best described through capabilities rather than arbitrary turn numbers. That is a recommendation for D002, not an already approved phase-transition rule. A threatened developed civilization may return to survival priorities without losing its technology or being reclassified as primitive.

## Bin 1 — game start: establish a viable foothold

**Player question:** What do I have, what can this place support, and what must I do first to keep a useful colony alive?

**Recommended boundary:** from the opening setup to a functioning settlement that can feed/support its people, perform useful work, and respond to immediate local threats. This is distinct from the current development task of making the AI work on the first map.

| ID / usefulness / status | Actionable idea | Why it belongs here; what useful evidence looks like |
|---|---|---|
| GS01 • Essential • Decision, opening details open | Make the opening legible: inspect terrain, usable yields, existing people, workplaces, equipment, technologies and nearby danger; turn visible opportunities into concrete candidate goals. | The recurring pleasure is noticing something worth doing and understanding how to do it. Evidence: an opportunity can be traced through requirements to an achievable action. The precise landing force/site setup is D001. Q001–Q002, Q021; S2, S4. |
| GS02 • Essential • Decision | Give the six outputs distinct jobs: Food supports people/growth; Minerals represent raw material; Industry manufacture/construction; Energy operation/training and selected other costs; Science thinking; Culture land/training and later collective advancement. Show stock, production, consumption and net change separately. | Stops nominal wealth from hiding a missing operating input. Evidence: the player and AI can identify the actual shortage and a real remedy. No free magical conversion; trade requires a partner. Q017, Q022–Q024, Q077. Earlier Credits/Research/Influence vocabulary is historical. |
| GS03 • Essential • Decision | Keep each population cohort one persistent entity with one current task/state: tile work, building work, construction, training, mobile service or combat. Growth creates useful capacity; casualties remove invested capacity. | People are the shared constraint linking every system. Evidence: reallocating or mobilizing changes output and availability without duplication; losses require ordinary growth/infrastructure to replace. Q025, Q031, Q035–Q039; S2. |
| GS04 • Essential • Decision / provisional shortage order | Establish food recovery, operating-input recovery and sensible productive defaults. Unassigned people work useful tiles when possible; food shortages prompt real food production; energy shortages can disable demanding installations. | GS05 • Essential • Decision | Use a weighted pool of proposals: advisers identify useful goals, governors develop local opportunities, the general assesses threats, and the leader authorizes compatible work and resolves conflicts. | This is the organizing principle across all four bins. Q003–Q004, Q010–Q016, Q055, Q127; S7. |
| GS06 • Essential • Decision | Give goals explicit objectives, blockers and enabling steps. | GS07 • Essential • Provisional defence policy | Establish a baseline garrison, a usable shelter concept and correct threat reach. | GS09 • High • Decision | Distinguish terrain, relief, features, deposits, improvements, districts and roads. Keep actual movement, yields and legal work access understandable; a district replaces natural extraction but can have a cost-bearing unworked benefit. | Geography should suggest choices before complexity multiplies. Evidence: replacing a tile has a visible opportunity cost, and visual labels agree with real effects. Q054, Q058, Q063, Q065, Q068; S4–S5. |
| GS10 • High • Recommendation | Present the first facilities as limited landing systems and locally demonstrated methods, with a small set of useful workplaces rather than a building catalogue. | S3/S4's distinction between inherited knowledge and manufacturable capability fits your practical research preference. | GS11 • Essential • Decision, applies throughout | Make decisions inspectable and reproducible: plan status, target, assigned means, blocker, rejection reason and reconsideration condition. Maintain deterministic decisions given equivalent relevant state and controlled randomness. | Diagnose why useful actions fail to happen. A reproducible battle is not proof of good tactics. Use a simple ordinary view and an inspectable detailed view. Q114–Q124, Q127; S7–S10. |

**Recommendation for this bin:** protect the coherent loop first. Do not make the opening require freight, elaborate diplomacy, every profession, a complete ecology simulation or a procedural world generator. A simple opening can still have many possible responses because its few systems interact.

**Capability checkpoint for discussion:** a civilization can explain its needs, keep a productive base operating, maintain basic safety and complete at least one chosen improvement/capability path through normal means. No fixed turn deadline is proposed.

## Bin 2 — early game: grow a protected network

**Player question:** Which nearby opportunities deserve people, infrastructure and protection, and how do I make expansion deliver more than empty markers?

**Recommended boundary:** sustained development beyond the initial foothold, with multiple useful sites, purposeful connections, basic trade/contact and the ability to organize a viable local operation. It need not mean every civilization must found the same number of towns.

| ID / usefulness / status | Actionable idea | Why it belongs here; what useful evidence looks like |
|---|---|---|
| E01 • Essential • Decision | Expand for useful yields, interesting land or operational position. A founding proposal includes real population, materials, a road plan and an escort that can become the garrison. Avoid worthwhile free income from one-person town spam. | Makes expansion an investment rather than an exploit. Evidence: a town develops useful capacity and its full cost competes with improving the existing town. Poor-yield support sites may still be valuable. Q018–Q019, Q049–Q050, Q055. |
| E02 • High • Decision | Give each tile one settlement working-area assignment at a time, with explicit transfer. Buy additional tiles as part of expansion plans, not merely to drain Culture. | Establishes responsibility for local development and prevents governors fighting over the same workforce opportunity. Evidence: overlap and transfers preserve legal one-worker-per-tile use. Q053–Q054. |
| E03 • Essential • Decision | Connect towns and useful outposts promptly through purposeful roads; roads need no ongoing upkeep at this stage. Do not reward blanket road coverage as a goal in itself. | Roads give investment a spatial purpose. Evidence: routes serve actual destinations and use real workers/costs; requests are not counted as completed connections. Q049, Q074–Q075. |
| E04 • High • Decision / conditional richer routing | Make transport primarily useful for fast group movement, initially off-road speed and rudimentary protection. Distinguish passenger movement from physical freight. Consider safety and terrain alongside travel time where practical. | Group movement should change choices, not add compulsory clicks to every trip. | E05 • Essential • Decision | Preserve useful local employment and train when a workplace or real plan creates demand. | E06 • High • Decision | Develop and clear land according to its purpose: restore valuable output, remove actual construction obstacles or reduce animal support near a threatened site. | E07 • High • Decision for first trade direction | Start external exchange with resources traded while a visitor is present; basic trading need not require the trained Merchant profession. | E08 • High • Recommendation | Give map opportunities three clear forms: renewable workplace, access-enabling deposit and one-time discovery. | E09 • Essential • Decision / provisional sizing | General proposes threat-removal plans; leader ranks them against other needs. | E10 • Essential • Provisional staging; decision on coordination | Assemble the initial force at the closest suitable settlement/outpost near the threat and depart in full. A lieutenant coordinates its assigned task force; it can be small, one unit, or eliminated. General remains the planning role. | Converts force ownership into coordinated action. | E12 • Essential • Decision / testing needed | Defend first, attack when likely to win, retreat and rebuild a larger force when it is not. Defer exact stacking, interception, zones of control and retreat-direction rules to tactical trials. | Makes an expedition serve an objective instead of endless attrition. | E13 • High • Decision | Make local wildlife resource-driven and species-dependent. | E14 • Conditional • Recommendation | Add a minimal defensive-site family: shelter/fortification plus observation, with clear separate effects. **Recommendation for this bin:** expansion, trade and expedition preparation should be alternate ways of answering current opportunities and shortages. Do not define early success solely as defeating a nest. A player that sensibly defers an unaffordable attack may be behaving well; a player that cannot formulate any useful enabling action is a different problem.

**Capability checkpoint for discussion:** the civilization can maintain useful development across sites, establish a meaningful connection or exchange, and prepare/execute a context-appropriate operation without contradicting its own staffing and defence needs. Exact scenario goals remain separately approved.

## Bin 3 — middle game: specialize and change methods

**Player question:** Which combinations of geography, population and technology should make this civilization distinctive, and which new constraints deserve investment?

**Recommended boundary:** a functioning network can support deliberate specialization, advanced services and alternative methods. Growth continues, but new capabilities matter more than repeating the opening on additional tiles.

| M02 • High • Decision / scale details open | Make growth beyond some thresholds require infrastructure; later concentration brings pollution, disease, squalor or related pressures with workable remedies. **Recommended boundary:** established industrial and institutional capacity can support long projects with ideological, ecological or technological meaning. Earlier land, population and security investments should still matter.

## Tensions to resolve before comparing with the build

1. **Game start versus early game:** what exactly arrives at landing, and is choosing the first site a player task? Stage definitions depend on that.
2. **Local Food versus pooled support:** local shortages would change staffing, migration and campaign budgeting. They can be designed without cargo simulation.
3. **Healthy expansion versus free-income spam:** passive districts, autonomous facilities and settlement baselines need compatible costs and useful-population demands.
4. **Working prerequisites versus research deadlocks:** functional facilities and working specialists need attainable entry routes. Some professions do not have a simple matching yield.
5. **Stable commitments versus useful reassignment:** current productive work deserves protection, but emergencies, completed tasks and obsolete purposes must release people.
6. **Full initial departure versus later reinforcements:** gathering the original expedition and feeding replacements are different phases. A task force also needs a definition of current adequate strength after losses.
7. **Fixed garrisons versus assessed threats:** a simple baseline is intended; numbers and frontier reinforcement rules still need testing.
8. **Civilian shelter versus actual protection:** occupancy, blocking, interception and zones of control must not be conflated. Q080's movement example still lacks a fully explicit destination/protection rule.
9. **A 25% power margin versus winning:** summing enemy power is a useful first estimator, but composition, terrain, action timing and enemy replacement economy can invalidate a simple ratio.
10. **Evolving ecology versus containable danger:** autonomous nest growth needs resource limits and readable species behaviour before uncontrolled expansion is useful.
11. **Stored experience versus obsolete evidence:** aggregate findings need build/context qualification and retirement of invalid assumptions; raw-run retention is deliberately bounded.
12. **Ideological endgame versus undefined victory:** the game has an intended destination, but its first concrete ambitions and completion conditions are not chosen yet.

These are not accusations of contradiction. Most are interfaces between individually sensible answers. Clarifying those interfaces should prevent the later implementation plan becoming another collection of disconnected fixes.
